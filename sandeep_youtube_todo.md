# YouTube Video Ideas

## Series that would build an audience fast

**"Discover It Yourself" — math and CS concepts taught through guided questions**
One concept per video. Never tell the viewer the answer upfront. Ask a sequence of questions and pause. Area of a rectangle → triangle → why π appears in a circle. Gradient descent discovered by trying to minimize error manually. This is your core philosophy on video — nobody else is doing this at scale.

**"I asked AI to teach me X — here's where it failed"**
You understand both sides: what good teaching looks like, and how LLMs actually work. A series where you probe AI tutors on mathematics, catch the moments they skip the struggle, and show what a better sequence would look like. Genuinely original angle.

**"Behind the paper"**
Walk through your own research — the PCA embeddings paper, future work — in plain language. What was the question? What did you try that failed? What does the result actually mean for someone building a retrieval system? Most researchers cannot explain their own work to a general audience. You can.

## High-shareability standalone videos

- **"Why the C++ compiler is written in C++"** — the Bjarne Stroustrup story you already wrote. Visual, surprising, leads naturally into recursion and bootstrapping. Perfect first video.
- **"H.C. Verma wrote back"** — read the letter on camera. The story of a village kid writing to an IIT professor is deeply relatable across India.
- **"Every club rejected me at IIT Roorkee — so I built my own"** — the IMG story. Founders and students will share this widely.
- **"What Amazon taught me that business school doesn't"** — the writing culture, the six-pager, thinking in documents. This gets shared in corporate circles.
- **"Is it still worth learning to code?"** — your actual answer is more nuanced and interesting than the usual takes. You've taught hundreds of thousands of people. Your answer has weight.

## For the educator and founder audience

- **"How I taught the Pythagorean theorem without stating it"** — live demonstration of a discovery sequence with a real learner or with the viewer as the learner
- **"Why enterprise AI demos lie"** — concrete, opinionated, will be shared by people who've been burned
- **"What 10 years of teaching online taught me about how people actually learn"** — not theory, observations from CloudxLab data and student interactions
- **"The moment I quit my Amazon job to start a teaching company"** — personal, specific, high emotional resonance
