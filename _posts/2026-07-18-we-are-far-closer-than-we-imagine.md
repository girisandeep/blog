Do our parents teach us to walk? Did our parents give us the skills to learn a language? We did learn language from our family and society, but the capability to learn language was already within us. We already had the infrastructure for it.

Were we blank slates — *tabula rasa*? No, not at all. We are born with all the infrastructure needed to learn language, or at least the building blocks that grow and develop into that infrastructure.

So we understand what it means to love, to have family, to belong to a people — even if no one explicitly teaches us. We are born with the infrastructure for all of this.

A child inherits some traits from the mother, some from the father, and some through random variation. That random selection occurs only in features where the mother and father differ — nature's way of determining what is essential and what is incidental.

Over the course of a lifetime, our experiences leave faint molecular traces on our DNA — biological echoes of what we faced, passed on to our children through epigenetics. The parents' DNA is itself the product of what they were born with, plus whatever their lives inscribed on them before the child was born.

And this chain has continued without interruption.

You are the outcome of your own learning since childhood, your parents' learning, their parents' learning, and so on — all the way back. You have, in a sense, lived the lives of billions of people. Your mind is the distillation of billions of years of human experience, shaped by millions of years of evolution.

---

Now, let us do some simple arithmetic.

You have two parents. Together they had four parents. Those four had eight. And so on.

Can you guess how many ancestors you had just 100 generations back — roughly 2,500 years ago?

$$2^{100}$$

That is approximately a 1 followed by 30 zeros. The number of ancestors you theoretically had in just the last 2,500 years.

Of course, that number is far greater than the total number of humans who have ever walked the Earth. This means our family trees cannot expand outward forever — their branches loop back, overlap, and merge. The tree collapses in on itself. The same ancestors appear again and again, shared across what we think of as entirely separate lineages.

---

What this tells us is that everyone alive today is, in a very real sense, your family. We all share common ancestors — not millions of years ago, but mere thousands of years ago.

We are far closer to one another than we typically imagine.

And somewhere deep inside, we know this. When we see a baby — any baby, regardless of whose it is — something in us shifts. We sense, without being told, that we are kin.
